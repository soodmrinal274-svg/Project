6th Century BC • c. 560 BC Ancient Coins Vault

The Lydian Lion Stater

📍 Excavation Site: Sardis, Ancient Lydia (Modern-day Turkey)
⏳ Historical Era: Archaic Greek / Mermnad Dynasty

Historical Context: Considered the world's earliest state-guaranteed sovereign coinage. Struck in electrum (a natural gold-silver alloy) during King Croesus's reign, the facing lion emblem certified official purity and revolutionized world trade by replacing primitive barter systems.

EXCAVATION & ARCHAEOLOGICAL DOSSIER • ARCHIVE #HCM-AC-01

The Lydian Lion Stater (Electrum Coinage)

Curated & Verified by Mrinal
Catalog ID: AC-560-LID
Century / Era: 6th Century BC (c. 560 BC)
Excavation Site: Sardis Royal Riverbed, Kingdom of Lydia
Material: Electrum (54% Gold, 44% Silver, 2% Copper)
Weight / Diameter: 10.82 grams • 19 mm
Preservation State: Museum Grade I (Extremely Fine)

Historical Significance & Provenance

Discovered during the 1912 American Expedition to Sardis along the gold-bearing sands of the Pactolus river. King Croesus standardized weights and alloy ratios, stamping the royal crest—a roaring lion confronting a bull—onto heated electrum blanks using engraved bronze punches.

Prior to this innovation, merchants were forced to test, weigh, and calculate purity on balances for every marketplace transaction. The Lydian Stater created trust in civic governance, laying the groundwork for classical Mediterranean economy and modern sovereign fiat.

18th Century BC • c. 1750 BC Ancient Civilizations

Basalt Stele of Hammurabi's Code

📍 Excavation Site: Susa, Khuzestan (Elamite Capital, Iran)
⏳ Historical Era: Old Babylonian Empire

Historical Context: A towering black diorite stele engraved with 282 legal edicts in Akkadian cuneiform. Hammurabi is depicted receiving the scepter and ring of divine justice from Shamash, establishing judicial precedent and legal transparency.

EXCAVATION & ARCHAEOLOGICAL DOSSIER • ARCHIVE #HCM-AC-02

Stele of Hammurabi's Code (Legal Monument)

Curated & Verified by Mrinal
Catalog ID: AC-1750-BAB
Century / Era: 18th Century BC (c. 1750 BC)
Excavation Site: Susa Acropolis, Iran (recovered 1901)
Material: Carved Black Basalt / Diorite
Dimensions: Height 2.25 m • Circumference 1.65 m
Script: Old Babylonian Cuneiform in Semitic Akkadian

Historical Significance & Provenance

Carried off as war booty by the Elamite King Shutruk-Nakhunte in the 12th century BC, this monumental pillar stood for millennia until uncovered by French archaeologist Gustave Jéquier.

The inscription addresses commercial liability, agricultural leasing, medical malpractice, family law, and the famous principle of proportionality (lex talionis). It represents humanity's earliest surviving comprehensive civil legal codification.

The scene captures a rare psychological pause amidst the bloody Homeric war: Achilles calls out "four" and Ajax calls out "three" on the game board. Their spears remain poised, balancing martial urgency with intimate stillness.

Also entombed within the resin bubble are two extinct Proplebeia dominicana worker bees caught in the act of pollination.

📍 Excavation Site: Great Namaqualand Desert, Hardap (Namibia)
⏳ Historical Era: Protoplanetary Core / Primordial Solar System

Historical Context: An extraterrestrial fragment forged inside the molten metallic core of a shattered planetesimal. Exhibiting pristine acid-etched Widmanstätten crystal structures that require millions of years of vacuum cooling to crystallize.

EXCAVATION & ARCHAEOLOGICAL DOSSIER • ARCHIVE #HCM-NH-12

Gibeon Iron-Nickel Octahedrite Meteorite Specimen

Curated & Verified by Mrinal
Catalog ID: NH-4.5GYA-MET
Primordial Age: 4.56 Billion Years Old
Strewnfield Location: Gibeon Strewnfield, Namibia (Impact ~12,000 BP)
Class / Group: Fine Octahedrite (IVA) Iron Meteorite
Composition: 91.8% Fe, 7.9% Ni, 0.4% Co, trace Gallium/Germanium
Specimen Mass: 42.5 kg • Polished & Nital Etched Face

Historical Significance & Provenance

The interlocking Kamacite and Taenite crystalline lamellae (Widmanstätten pattern) cannot be replicated in any laboratory on Earth, as they require cooling at a rate of merely 1°C every 100,000 years in the cold vacuum of interplanetary space.

Indigenous Nama tribesmen used fragments of this celestial metal to forge high-tensile spear points centuries before Western explorers documented the strewn field in 1836.
